You do not need a lift and a paint gauge to learn a lot about a car. These five details take ten minutes and tell you whether a car has been cared for.

## 1. Panel gaps and reflections

Stand at each corner and look down the flank. Reflections should run cleanly from panel to panel. Uneven gaps or a sudden change in the reflection often mean repair work.

## 2. Tyres

Four matching tyres of a quality brand suggest an owner who did not cut corners. Check the date codes, too: old rubber can look perfect and still be unsafe.

## 3. The driver’s seat bolster

It is the first surface to show wear. Compare it with the mileage. A worn bolster on a low-mileage car deserves a question.

## 4. Glass

Original glass carries the manufacturer’s marking. A replaced windscreen is normal; replaced side glass raises a question about why.

## 5. The paperwork, in order

A thick folder is not the point. What matters is a continuous story: services at sensible intervals, invoices that match the book, and no unexplained gaps.

If a seller is reluctant to let you look at any of these, that tells you something too.
